Her Story

Who She Was

Her full name was Karemaa Soliman Mohamed Hadhoud.

She was born on March 22, 1952 (26 Jumada al-Thani 1371 AH) in Mashala, Santa, Gharbia Governorate, Egypt.
She passed into God's mercy on March 29, 2019 (22 Rajab 1440 AH).

Her Childhood

She grew up in simplicity and dignity in the village of Mashala, Gharbia Governorate, in a household known for its strength and honor. Though family circumstances kept her from completing her formal education so she could help support her family and care for her younger siblings, she stood out from childhood for her sharp mind and rare insight, ahead of many around her. In the prime of her youth, she faced a severe health trial after a medical error left her blind in one eye — yet she never surrendered or weakened. She turned that trial into strength, growing only more resolute, steadfast, and certain in God's decree.

Her Family

She was the wife of Al-Azzab Shebl Al-Azzab, may God have mercy on them both, with whom she had three sons: Sherif, Hisham, and Mahmoud. She was born to her father, Soliman Mohamed Hadhoud, and her mother, Amina Mostafa Neda. Her siblings are Hany Soliman Mohamed Hadhoud (may God have mercy on him), Adel Soliman Mohamed Hadhoud (may God have mercy on him), Ibrahim Soliman Mohamed Hadhoud, and Madiha Soliman Mohamed Hadhoud.

Important Life Milestones

Her life was defined by milestones of devotion and wise stewardship. From an early age, when her brothers joined the military and others traveled abroad, she courageously stepped up as the sole pillar of the family, taking full responsibility for running the household and managing the family’s agricultural land. Following her marriage, she moved to Cairo, bringing with her the vision, wisdom, and generosity that helped purchase the land and establish the "Family Home" in Nasr City—turning it into a warm sanctuary and gathering place for all her children and grandchildren. In her later years, she set a profound example of patience and resilience as she fought pulmonary fibrosis and the weight of illness with a minimal lung capacity that astounded doctors. Until her final days, she remained steadfast and dignified, wearing her shawl and attending to her needs with pride, steadfastly refusing to be a burden to anyone.

Her Personality

A truly extraordinary woman, she carried both dignity and courage, ambition and a masterful hand for managing life. She was the family's compass and its problem-solver, meeting even the hardest circumstances with a smile and unshakeable hope. Though she never attended school, she managed community savings groups, rent, and financial obligations herself, and taught herself to write letters so she could keep her accounts with remarkable precision. She had a heart wide enough for everyone, and an uncanny gift for holding her children and grandchildren close with wit and unforgettable tenderness. She remained devoted to her siblings despite the distance, faithful to her prayers, listening to the Qur'an and tracing its verses with her hand in reverence and humility. Despite everything she endured, despair never found its way to her heart — she remained smiling, at peace with God's will, until the very last day of her life.

Things She Loved

She cherished abundance and careful stewardship, and despised waste. Her skilled hands made the most delicious stuffed vegetables, and trays of liver and cake unlike any other. She found her happiness in a warm bowl of sherya bel laban (vermicelli in warm milk), in the quiet stillness of dawn, and in gathering her children and grandchildren around her at holidays and family occasions. She loved keeping her grandchildren company over games of cards, and never hesitated to raise whatever birds or pets they asked for, just to please them. Her heart was tied to the words of God; she took joy in listening to the Qur'an and tracing its letters with her hand, longing for closeness to God and His blessing. Everything she loved was, in truth, an echo of her own spirit: simplicity, generosity, and closeness to God and family.

Known For

Known throughout her neighborhood, young and old alike, as "Umm Sherif," she was famous for her boundless generosity and an open home that no one ever left without being honored and made to feel cared for. She had an unmatched touch in the kitchen, especially her stuffed vegetables, her liver and giblets, and her unforgettable cake — alongside a reputation for devotion, faithfully keeping her prayers, remembrance, and glorification of God.

Important Memories

Our memory is rich with unforgettable moments: from the grandkids sneaking down to her apartment on school nights, to her proud ululations that filled the house with joy for every success, her wise way of handling the children's mischief, and her enduring devotion to keeping family bonds alive with her siblings in the village despite the distance. Her spirit remains vividly present in every corner she left behind.

Grandma "Karima" passed away on a blessed Friday in 2019, bid farewell by a vast crowd of loved ones that reflected her generosity and pure legacy. She passed before witnessing the various milestones of my life, yet I know that her joyous ululations from every childhood award still echo in my soul. Today, whenever people tell me I resemble her in features, character, or strength, I feel immense pride, knowing that her blessings, prayers, and living spirit will walk alongside me through every journey of success.

Things She Left in Us

Her Famous Saying

"Ya nahar mesh aswad" ("No day is truly black")

Her constant, instinctive reply to any hint of despair, planting hope and acceptance in even the hardest moments. She said it in anger and in boredom just as she said it in joy and in wonder — as if it were a standing promise between her and life that no day would ever be dark in her eyes, whatever it carried.

Our Late-Night Refuge
We'd ask her to call for us from the courtyard, and she'd answer lovingly. Warm evenings away from the strictness of bedtime, spent laughing, playing cards, and watching TV until midnight, entirely safe under her watch.

Tears of Shared Love
When I was upset and crying as a child, she would comfort me tenderly and say, "Alright, habiba — if you can't hold it in and calm down, I'll cry with you." And when I couldn't stop, she truly would start crying with me — a gesture that could only come from a heart that loves and feels for others, completely and sincerely.

The Dove's Praise
We woke every day to the sound of the mourning dove, and she would ask me lovingly, "Do you know what they're saying?" I'd answer, "No, I don't know..." and she'd say, "They're saying wahhidoh (glorify Him alone)..." Then together we'd say, "There is no god but God."

Details That Lit Up Her Heart
A warm bowl of milk noodles on winter nights, the serene stillness of dawn, and having her grandchildren gathered around her during holidays—simple things that filled her heart with pure joy.

The Secret Word
Her singular gift for melting away a child's mischief with one magic phrase: whispering "Amoura el-Amoura" to my gentle, mischievous brother, and within seconds his anger would dissolve into calm — and a quiet "astaghfirullah" under his breath, amid her warm smile.

Steadfast Acceptance and Resolve
She taught us that the bitterness of fate isn't answered with resentment, but with resolve and acceptance — she who faced the loss of sight in one eye, and years of illness and exhaustion, and never once lost her smile. She taught us that a person is measured by their mind, their wisdom, their generosity, and the mark they leave on others — and that good faith in God is the truest weapon against any trial.
